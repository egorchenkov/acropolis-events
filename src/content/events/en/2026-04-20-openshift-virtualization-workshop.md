On April 20, 2026, the ACROPOLIS INTEGRO team delivered the **OpenShift Virtualization Roadshow** — a one-day hands-on workshop for virtualization admins, DevOps engineers, and architects who want to master VM management on Red Hat's modern Kubernetes platform.

The format was designed not as a product pitch but as a working lab — with a real environment deployed and step-by-step drills through the scenarios teams face when moving to OpenShift Virtualization.

### What we covered in one day

- How to unify virtual machines and containers in a single OpenShift infrastructure
- Creating, migrating, and managing VMs in OpenShift Virtualization
- **Migration Toolkit for Virtualization (MTV)** — the tool for migrating VMs from VMware
- Working with VM templates, networks, and storage
- VM backup and recovery practices in OpenShift
- Day-2 operations and publishing applications running inside VMs

### Format

The workshop combines theory and hands-on practice — participants apply what they learn in a live environment immediately. A step-by-step flow: architecture → deployment → migration → operations. Seats were limited, which let us keep an individual pace and answer questions as they came up.

### Who benefits from this training

- Virtualization admins evaluating alternatives to VMware
- DevOps engineers consolidating VM and container workloads
- Infrastructure architects designing hybrid Kubernetes environments
- Teams planning workload migration to OpenShift

### Outcome for participants

Each participant gained hands-on experience with OpenShift Virtualization in a real environment and a clear picture of how to design a migration of their own VM estate. For continued self-study — the official [OpenShift Virtualization Roadshow guide](https://rhpds.github.io/openshift-virt-roadshow-cnv-multi-user/modules/index.html).

### Next steps

A pilot rollout of OpenShift Virtualization in customer infrastructures and detailed migration planning from VMware via MTV. Further sessions are planned on Day-2 operations, backup, and disaster recovery for VMs in OpenShift.
